# Questions to Ask Your Interviewers

> Strategic questions for the "do you have any questions for me?" phase — where Principal candidates demonstrate strategic thinking, org awareness, and genuine curiosity. Adapt to the conversation; never read from a script.

---

## The One Question You MUST Ask Every Interviewer

**"What does success look like for this role in the first 6 months?"**

Non-negotiable. Shows outcome-orientation, reveals real expectations, and lets you calibrate your answers.
- Hiring Manager: "What are the top 3 deliverables you'd expect by month 6?"
- Peer: "What would make you think 'I'm glad we hired this person' by month 6?"
- Director: "What business outcome should IBTE deliver that I'm directly responsible for?"

---

## Questions by Interviewer Type

### Hiring Manager
- **"What's the biggest architectural challenge on the IBTE platform right now?"** — Reveals real pain points. Follow up: "How are you approaching it today?"
- **"How do you measure engineering success at the org level?"** — DORA, business outcomes, NPS? Tells you how your work is evaluated.
- **"How does engineering get a seat at the table for product decisions?"** — Ticket-taking orgs are a red flag at Principal level.

### Peer Engineer
- **"What's the team's approach to incident management and post-mortems?"** — Listen for blameless culture, action items tracked to closure.
- **"How do you handle technical debt? Formal process or ad-hoc?"** — Look for budgets, dedicated sprints, or review boards.
- **"What's the worst production incident in the last 6 months and what changed?"** — Tests incident maturity. Blame-only teams are a firefighting trap.

### Product / Stakeholder
- **"How do you prioritize platform investments vs feature delivery?"** — Good answer names a framework (RICE, WSJF). Push for the percentage split.
- **"What's the biggest frustration your team has with the IBTE platform today?"** — Raw feedback that becomes your first backlog item.

### Director / VP
- **"What's the technical vision for IBTE in the next 3 years?"** — Coherent = strong leadership. Vague = you'll be inventing it yourself.
- **"How does IBTE fit into The Standard's broader insurance technology strategy?"** — Reveals whether IBTE is strategic or a cost center.
- **"What's the biggest organizational challenge you're facing with engineering?"** — Tells you your non-technical job: talent, retention, culture, or process.

### HR / Recruiter
- **"What's the on-call expectation for Principal Engineers?"** — Escalation-only vs regular rotation. Get the real answer.
- **"How is performance evaluated for Principal Engineers?"** — If they can't articulate it, they may not understand the level.
- **"What's the retention rate on the IBTE team and what do people leave for?"** — Team health signal. High churn is worth investigating.

## Questions to AVOID

| Category | Example | Why |
|----------|---------|-----|
| Too junior | "What tech stack do you use?" | Should know from research. |
| Too selfish | "What's the promotion path from Principal?" | Terminal role. Save comp for HR. |
| Too generic | "What's the culture like?" | Ask specifics about decision-making or autonomy. |
| Already answered | "What's the role about?" | They just spent 45 minutes on it. |
| Too negative | "How do you innovate in a slow industry?" | Rephrase: "How do you balance compliance with delivery speed?" |
| Negotiation too early | Salary / remote policy | Save for the offer stage. |

---

## How to Read the Room

**When to ask:** Pick 2-3 questions, not all 7.
- Technical conversation → technical question (incidents, debt, architecture).
- Strategic conversation → strategic question (vision, org challenges).
- Warm conversationalist → "worst production incident" or "biggest frustration" — builds rapport.
- Interviewer wrapping up → The One Question. Clean, powerful closer.

**When to save:**
- Money, benefits, remote → save for HR screen.
- Team politics sensed during interview → trust your gut, factor into offer decision.
- Questions already answered → listen carefully.
- Interviewer is rushed or late → just The One Question and thanks.

**The "No Questions" Trap:** NEVER say "I think you covered everything." Signals disengagement. Always have The One Question ready.

### Suggested 3-Question Shortlist (Works for Any Round)

1. "What does success look like for this role in the first 6 months?"
2. (Hiring Manager / Peer) "What's the biggest architectural challenge on the IBTE platform?"
3. (Director / VP) "What's the technical vision for IBTE in the next 3 years?"

# Face-to-Face Interview Script — Start to End (Director Round, 11 Aug)

> Full script for speaking in the in-person interview: every high-stakes moment has word-for-word lines; the middle is governed by the answer machine. Built for the "10 years of virtual, first F2F in a decade" gap.
> Companion: `00_director_level_playbook.md`, `01_director_qa_scripts.md`, `02_questions_to_ask_director.md`, `03_research_findings.md`.

---

## Phase 0 — Before entering (30 min prior)

- Water, light snack, bathroom. Phone on **silent**, in bag — never in hand.
- One-page cheat sheet in a slim folder: Top 20 numbers + 3 anchor one-liners + your 3 questions. Glancing at it = prepared, not lost.
- Say the opening script (Phase 3) aloud **once**.
- Box breathing at reception: 4s in → 4s hold → 4s out → 4s hold. Twice.

---

## Phase 1 — Reception (2 min)

> Line: **"Good morning. I'm Chokkar Gurusamy — I have an interview at 10 AM. Here's my confirmation email."**

- Have the email ready (phone or printed). Show it.
- Be warm with security/reception. Someone reports on how you treated them.

---

## Phase 2 — First meeting: handshake + walk (2-3 min)

- Stand, smile, firm handshake (2 pumps), eye contact: **"Nice to meet you, Nivedha. Thank you for having me — the office is beautiful. Is the whole GCC based in this building?"**
- Match their small-talk pace. 2-3 exchanges max. Do NOT launch into interview mode. Do NOT complain about traffic.

---

## Phase 3 — The opening (first ~2 minutes)

When they say *"So, Chokkar, tell me about yourself"*: pause 2 seconds, breathe, sit forward. Then:

> "Thank you — happy to. I'm a Staff Engineer at Dish Network with 14+ years building and architecting enterprise platforms, and my last four years have been at the intersection of large-scale systems and AI-augmented delivery. Three things define my career.
>
> **First, architecture ownership at scale:** I own the architecture for Dish's subscriber platform — 10 million subscribers, ninety-nine point nine percent uptime, sub-second latency.
>
> **Second, org-wide modernization with business impact:** I led the Spring Boot 3 migration across 12 platform services — 50,000+ lines — compressing an 18-month estimate into 5 months, and I was promoted to Staff Engineer for it.
>
> **Third, practices that outlive me:** I created AI agent skills and MCP integrations that 5+ engineering teams adopted org-wide — now a standard at Dish — and that work earned two consecutive Technical Excellence Awards.
>
> I'm here because the CIO said the BenTech integration API center of excellence lives in this office — and defining those patterns from day one is exactly the scope I operate at."

**Delivery rules:** slow down on "ninety-nine point nine", "sub-second", "eighteen months into five". Eye contact across the panel. Sit forward, hands on table. Stop at the end — let them react.

---

## Phase 4 — The body (45-60 min): the answer machine

Every answer = 4 steps (from `../../talks/30`):
1. **BLUF** — conclusion in one sentence
2. **Signpost** — "Three things: X, Y, Z"
3. **Stack mechanics** — only when asked for depth
4. **Metric** — the number with its measurement story

### Hard question (silence is your friend)
> "That's a great question — let me take a moment." → 3-5 seconds of **thinking silence** (in person this reads as thoughtful, not broken). Then the 4 steps.

### Being challenged (never defend)
> "That's a fair push — we weighed exactly that. The trade-off was X. Here's the data that made the call right." → then the metric.

### Whiteboard (if offered)
- Stand, take the pen, pause. Draw the **frame first**: 3 boxes (Ingress → Core → Data), or problem on the left → outcome on the right.
- **Talk before you write, then draw. Never talk while writing** — face the room when speaking.
- Keep diagrams simple. The talk is the product.

### Reading the interviewer
| Signal | Move |
|---|---|
| Lean forward | Go deeper |
| Lean back / glance at watch | Cut to the metric, stop |
| Writing | Pause after your BLUF so they capture it |

---

## Phase 5 — Your questions (5-8 min)

- **"I have a couple of questions for you, if that's alright."** (Always yes.)
- Ask 2-3 from `02_questions_to_ask_director.md`, end with The One Question:
  > **"What does success look like for this role in the first six months — what business outcome should I be directly responsible for?"**
- Listen actively: nod, follow up once ("What's driving that timeline?").

---

## Phase 6 — The close (2 min)

> "Before we wrap — three things I want you to remember about me. **One:** I build patterns that scale across teams, proven at 10 million users. **Two:** I turn 18-month roadmaps into 5-month deliveries with business outcomes, not just output. **Three:** I'm here to define the integration architecture for this center from day one — not inherit it. I'd love to do that for The Standard."

Then:
- **"What are the next steps in the process?"** — legitimate, shows you want the job.
- Thank specifically: **"Thank you for the deep dive on the BenTech strategy — I'm even more convinced this is the right role."**
- Handshake, eye contact, hold the door for anyone behind you.

## The F2F vs Virtual Gap — 10 Things That Change

1. **Presence is physical now.** Virtual hid 80% of you. In person you're scored from the handshake: sit forward, open posture, hands visible, gesture when landing a point.
2. **Eye contact.** 60-70% of the time, swept across the panel — look at the questioner, then the others when you land the BLUF.
3. **Silence.** Virtual dead air felt like a dropped call, so you trained yourself to fill it with "um, so, yeah". In person, 3-5 seconds of thought is normal and reads as deliberate. **Use it.**
4. **Notes.** Virtual let you hide a screen. In person, a one-page folder with your numbers is acceptable — glance, don't read. Everyone at this level carries talking points.
5. **Voice.** Slower and slightly louder than feels natural. End BLUFs with a downward pitch. Pause after every number.
6. **Filler words kill in person.** Replace "um/so/like" with silence. (The 4-step answer standard is the anti-rambling device — your doc 30 feedback was "answers lacked structure"; in person, rambling is more visible and more damaging.)
7. **You're watched outside the room.** Courteous to everyone — security, reception, the person who brings water.
8. **Phone.** Never in the waiting area. Read your folder instead.
9. **Attire.** Formal shirt/trousers (GCC corporate standard) — comfortable enough that you never adjust it.
10. **The nerves fix.** Rehearse the opening 3x aloud in the morning, arrive early, box-breathe at reception. A scripted first 90 seconds kills 80% of the anxiety.

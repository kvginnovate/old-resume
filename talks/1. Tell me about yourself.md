## Full Cover Story (5 minutes)

> I'm a Staff Engineer at Dish Network Technologies, with 14 plus years of experience in backend architecture and microservices. Today I own the end to end architecture for the subscriber facing platform. That is the My Dish App platform, a set of Java and Spring Boot microservices running on Kubernetes that serves more than 10 million subscribers with 99.9 percent uptime and sub second API latency.
>
> My job has two halves. The first half is architecture ownership. I define the patterns, lead the design reviews, drive the technical direction, and write the decisions down as architecture decision records, so the whole organization builds the same way instead of inventing twenty different answers to the same problem. The second half is staying hands on. I still implement features, I still review code, and I still participate in incident response, because I do not believe an architect who has stopped writing code can make credible decisions about code.
>
> The work I am most proud of is introducing AI spec driven modernization at Dish. The problem was that we had decades of legacy code, and migrating it manually took months per system. I built a workflow where AI agents reverse engineer the legacy code, generate a specification of what the system actually does, and then automate the remediation of breaking changes against that specification. To make that work inside a real enterprise, I configured MCP servers in Kiro, our AI development environment, wiring the agents to internal tools like GitLab and Jira, so they could triage defects, open merge requests, and coordinate fixes across the delivery pipeline on their own. That approach was adopted company wide across multiple teams, and we used it to migrate more than 80 legacy Ruby on Rails APIs to Java microservices, cutting delivery timelines by 70 percent.
>
> The same playbook produced our Spring Boot 3 migration. We moved 50,000 plus lines of code across platform services, automating the remediation of breaking changes, and we measured a 60 percent productivity gain. I also built a custom dashboard to track every line of AI committed code, so leadership could see progress instead of taking my word for it. On the quality side, I wired a Snyk MCP server to the agents so vulnerabilities get scanned and fixed the same day, and a Dynatrace MCP server that turns incident diagnosis from a 30 minute hunt into a two minute trace. And when our mobile team was struggling to debug issues, I configured a Crashlytics MCP server that gives the agents direct access to crash reports, and we used it to find and fix those issues far faster.

The lesson I generalized from that: when you are blocked on a mechanical problem, automate the mechanical part and have humans review the judgment calls.
>

We moved 50,000 plus lines of code across platform services, automating the remediation of breaking changes, and we measured a 60 percent productivity gain. I also built a custom dashboard that tracked every line of AI committed code, so leadership could see progress instead of taking my word for it. ## Delivery Tips

- **Start with the 2-minute version unless told otherwise.** Most interviewers ask "tell me about yourself" expecting 2 minutes. If they say "take your time" or look engaged and lean in, expand into the 5-minute version.
- **The If I Join section is the closer.** In the 10-minute speech it runs ~2 minutes before the Future paragraph. In the 5-minute version it's one paragraph. If the interviewer asks "how would you approach this role" or "what would your first 90 days look like", that standalone section is your full answer (cross-reference talks/23 for the 90-day detail).
- **The 10-minute speech is a special case.** Only give it if the interviewer explicitly asks for the full picture, or in a panel/onsite setting where the slot is scheduled for it. It now covers the entire resume — every Dish product line and every MSys client project gets airtime.
- **The versions are modular, not fixed scripts.** 10 minutes → drop the DPA and Kiro Mobile paragraphs to land near 10:30. 5 minutes → drop the Sling TV paragraph to land near 4:30. 2 minutes is the floor — below that you lose the story.
- **Pause after every number.** "10 million subscribers." Pause. "99.9 percent uptime." Pause. The numbers are the signal; rushing past them wastes them.
- **Vary energy by section.** Slow and steady for the present role, pick up energy on the AI modernization story, slow down again for the future section. The future section should feel like a landing, not a fade-out.
- **Do not read it.** This is a map, not a script. Memorize the section order and the numbers, then say it in your own words.
- **Watch for the hook.** The sentences most likely to trigger a follow-up are the MCP-in-Kiro sentence (technical curiosity), the worktrees sentence (how does that even work), and the GCP POC (can you really do that?). If the interviewer interrupts on any of them, stop the script and answer them.

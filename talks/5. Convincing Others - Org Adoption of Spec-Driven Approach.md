# 5. Convincing Others — Org Adoption of Spec-Driven Approach

**Target:** Principal Software Engineer V — The Standard (IBTE)
**Duration:** 55-65 seconds

---

## Question

**Interviewer:** "You said you led the technical direction without managing the team. That's a recurring theme — you influence decisions without authority. Tell me about a specific time you had to convince a skeptical team or leader to adopt your approach."

---

## Answer

I introduced AI spec driven modernization at Dish. The idea was simple: instead of manually reverse engineering legacy code, use AI agents to analyze the codebase, generate OpenAPI specifications, and then scaffold new microservices from those specs. The AI did the mechanical translation, we did the engineering.

I gave a demo to our VP of engineering. I showed the side by side comparison — one service migrated manually took 2 weeks, the same scope with the spec driven approach took 3 days with fewer defects. The VP was convinced.

I then gave the same demo to other teams across the org. Within a quarter, 5 teams were using the approach. We applied it to migrate 80 plus legacy Ruby on Rails APIs to Java microservices, cutting delivery timelines by 70 percent.

The key lesson was: don't ask people to trust your approach. Show them the data, let them decide. If the methodology is genuinely better, teams will pull it themselves.

## Key Signals

- Org influence without authority — no mandate, just data and demos
- VP-level engagement — you operate at the VP level naturally
- Evidence-driven — "3 days vs 2 weeks, fewer defects"
- Bottom-up adoption — "teams will pull it themselves"
- Principal-level insight — "Don't ask people to trust. Show them the data."

---

## Delivery Tips

- "I didn't ask people to adopt" — this reframes the whole answer. Shows you understand bottom-up adoption
- The key lesson line — "Don't ask people to trust your approach. Show them the data, let them decide" — deliver it as a conviction, not a summary
- Keep it tight — 60 seconds. This is a vignette, not a full migration narrative

---

## Follow-Up Questions

- **"Did anyone resist despite the data?"** — One team was skeptical because they had a failed migration tool experience. I offered to pair with their lead engineer on the first service. After seeing it work on their own codebase, they adopted it.
- **"How did you handle edge cases the AI couldn't handle?"** — I fixed the skill instead of asking each team to work around it. When a team reported an edge case, I updated the agent to handle it. This made the tool better for everyone.
- **"What was the hardest part of getting adoption?"** — Trust. Teams had seen automation tools fail before. Data was the only way to overcome skepticism. Nothing else worked.

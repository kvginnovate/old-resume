# 3. Why Are You Leaving Dish / Why The Standard

**Target:** Principal Software Engineer V — The Standard (IBTE)
**Duration:** 80-90 seconds

---

## Question

**Interviewer:** "You've been at Dish for 4 years with great results — awards, promotions, org-wide adoption. So why are you looking? What's drawing you to The Standard?"

---

## Answer

That's a fair question. I've had a great run at Dish — promoted to Staff Engineer, back-to-back Technical Excellence Awards, built things adopted org-wide. I'm proud of that work.

But I've reached a point where I want broader scope. At Dish, I own the architecture for the subscriber platform and Sling TV. It's one product line. I'm ready for a role where I drive technical strategy across multiple product lines — insurance, retirement, investments — each with its own domain complexity.

What drew me to The Standard is three things. First, the Bengaluru centre is brand new — this is a founding architecture role. I get to define how things are built from day one, not inherit someone else's decisions.

Second, insurance is a natural fit for event-driven architecture. Claims, underwriting, policy management — these are textbook event-driven workflows. I've been doing exactly that with Kafka at Dish, and I want to double down on it at a Principal level.

And third, I read the CIO's interview where he said The Standard is focused on solving the hardest problems of AI first — underwriting, claims, integration — not chatbots. That's exactly the kind of real AI work I've been building. I could have joined any Bangalore GCC, but I chose The Standard because the technical challenge is real and the leadership is clear about what they want to build.

---

## Structure

| Section | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Not running from something | Great run at Dish — promotion, awards, org-wide adoption | ~10 sec |
| Why leave — scope | One product line vs multiple product lines with domain complexity | ~15 sec |
| Reason 1 — Founding role | Bengaluru centre is new, define from day one | ~15 sec |
| Reason 2 — Domain fit | Insurance is event-driven, Kafka experience maps directly | ~20 sec |
| Reason 3 — AI vision | CIO's interview, real AI problems, not chatbots | ~20 sec |
| Closer | "I could have joined any GCC, but I chose The Standard" | ~10 sec |

---

## Key Signals

- Not running from something — frame it as growth, not dissatisfaction
- Principal-level scope — "multiple product lines, each with its own domain complexity"
- Company research — CIO quote, new GCC, shows homework
- Bridges to your experience — Kafka, event-driven, AI
- Intentional choice — "I could have joined any Bangalore GCC, but I chose The Standard"

---

## Delivery Tips

- Don't say "I'm leaving because..." — say "I'm looking because..." It's a subtle but important reframe
- The CIO quote is a power move — it shows deeper research than most candidates
- The last line — "I could have joined any Bangalore GCC, but I chose The Standard" — say it with conviction. Let it land
- Keep it positive throughout. Never sound like you're running away from Dish

---

## Follow-Up Questions

- **"What's the hardest part about starting at a new GCC?"** — Building the engineering culture. The patterns are easy to define, but getting teams to adopt them takes trust and proof. I'd start with a high-impact, low-risk migration to build credibility.
- **"What if Azure is a challenge for you?"** — Kubernetes is Kubernetes. The orchestration concepts are identical. The Azure-specific services (VNET, Private Link, AKS) follow the same cloud patterns I've used on AWS and GCP. I'm confident I'd be productive within the first month.

---

## Notes

- "IBTE" is internal jargon at The Standard — the interviewer knows it. Don't repeat it. Say "Bengaluru centre" instead.
- Don't mention regulatory/compliance unless asked — it adds complexity without adding signal.
- Keep the three reasons crisp. If rushed, drop Reason 2 (domain fit) and land on Reason 3 (AI vision) — it's the strongest differentiator.

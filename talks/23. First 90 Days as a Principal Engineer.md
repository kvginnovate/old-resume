# 23. First 90 Days as a Principal Engineer

**Target:** Principal Software Engineer V — The Standard (IBTE)
**Duration:** 90-100 seconds
**High-Probability Principal Question** — tests leadership maturity, empathy, and strategic execution.

---

## Question

**Interviewer:** "Walk me through your ideal first 90 days as a Principal Engineer here at The Standard."

---

## Answer

My approach to the first 90 days is structured around **Listen, Align, and Deliver**. I don't walk in on Day 1 declaring architectural refactors; I focus on understanding the domain, building trust, and locating high-leverage bottlenecks.

**Days 1–30: Listen & Map.**
My top priority is context gathering across three vectors: technical, organizational, and operational. I perform code and architecture reviews of core business paths—like policy underwriting or claims ingestion—to map technical debt and platform dependencies. Simultaneously, I hold 1-on-1s with tech leads, product managers, and enterprise architects to align on business goals, pain points, and current friction. I also shadow on-call rotations to see how our systems behave under operational strain.

**Days 31–60: Target & Execute Small Wins.**
Once I understand the domain, I focus on executing a high-impact, low-risk architectural improvement—for instance, standardizing an API contract, optimizing a slow event-pipeline bottleneck, or integrating automated AI guardrails into CI/CD. The goal here is building trust by shipping production value alongside the team, demonstrating that my architectural leadership elevates velocity rather than introducing bureaucracy.

**Days 61–90: Strategy & Scaling.**
With domain context and team trust established, I synthesize my findings into a multi-quarter technical roadmap aligned with enterprise goals. I establish or refine governance mechanisms—such as lightweight ADR (Architecture Decision Record) templates, automated security gates, or shared agentic AI skills—that empower teams to move fast autonomously.

By Day 90, I’m not just contributing code; I have established self-service standards and a clear technical vision that scales across multiple engineering squads.

---

## Structure

| Section | Content | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Mindset | Listen, Align, Deliver — avoid "hero architect" traps | ~15 sec |
| Days 1–30 | Deep dive: tech stack, 1-on-1s with PM/Leads, shadow on-call | ~25 sec |
| Days 31–60 | Ship a concrete, high-leverage win with the team | ~25 sec |
| Days 61–90 | Formulate long-term strategy, governance & self-service patterns | ~20 sec |
| Outcome | Scalable self-service standards and aligned roadmap by Day 90 | ~10 sec |

---

## Key Signals

- **Humility & Listener Mindset:** You don't prescribe solutions before understanding domain/legacy context.
- **Operational Reality:** Mentioning shadowing on-call shows you care about production reliability, not just high-level diagrams.
- **Quick Win Strategy:** Proves you can write code and deliver immediate value alongside teams to build credibility.
- **Enterprise Alignment:** Translating technical roadmaps into business OKRs.

---

## Delivery Tips

- Emphasize **"Listen before prescribing"**—this separates true Principal leaders from dogmatic architects.
- Highlight **building trust with squad leads and product managers** as early priority #1.

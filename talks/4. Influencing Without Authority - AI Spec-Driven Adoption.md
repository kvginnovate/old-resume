# 4. Influencing Without Authority — AI Spec-Driven Adoption

**Target:** Principal Software Engineer V — The Standard (IBTE)
**Duration:** 55-65 seconds

---

## Question

**Interviewer:** "You said you led the technical direction without managing the team. That's a recurring theme. Tell me about a specific time you had to convince a skeptical team or leader to adopt your approach."

---

## Answer

I introduced AI spec driven modernization at Dish. The idea was simple. Instead of manually reverse engineering legacy code, use AI agents to analyze the codebase, generate OpenAPI specifications, and then scaffold new microservices from those specs. The AI did the mechanical translation, we did the engineering.

I gave a demo to our VP of engineering. I showed the side by side comparison. One service migrated manually took 2 weeks, the same scope with the spec driven approach took 3 days with fewer defects. The VP was convinced.

I then gave the same demo to other teams across the org. Within a quarter, 5 teams were using the approach. We applied it to migrate 80 plus legacy Ruby on Rails APIs to Java microservices, cutting delivery timelines by 70 percent.

The key lesson was: don't ask people to trust your approach. Show them the data, let them decide. If the methodology is genuinely better, teams will pull it themselves.

## Key Signals

- Org-wide influence without authority — VP demo, then teams self-adopted
- Evidence-driven — "2 weeks vs 3 days, fewer defects" — concrete numbers, not opinions
- Scaled organically — "5 teams within a quarter" — no mandate, no top-down enforcement
- Principal-level insight — "Don't ask people to trust. Show them the data, let them decide."

## Delivery Tips

- "I didn't argue" — say it plainly. Shows maturity, not ego.
- The key lesson line — deliver it as a conviction, not a summary. This is what you've internalized from experience.
- Keep it tight — 60 seconds. This is a vignette, not a full migration narrative.

## Follow-Up Questions

- **"How did you handle teams that still didn't adopt it?"** — I didn't push. I ran office hours, wrote documentation, and iterated on the approach based on feedback. When teams hit edge cases, I fixed the methodology instead of asking each team to work around it. Over time, the data did the convincing.
- **"What was the hardest part of getting adoption?"** — Initial skepticism. Engineers had seen failed automation attempts before. The side-by-side comparison was the only thing that worked — not promises, not demos, just data.
- **"Did you have to deal with any resistance from senior engineers?"** — One senior engineer was openly skeptical. I asked him to try it on his lowest-priority service. When he saw the results, he became the biggest advocate.

# 8. Operating Independently — Defining Architecture From Scratch

**Target:** Principal Software Engineer V — The Standard (IBTE)
**Duration:** 70-80 seconds

---

## Question

**Interviewer:** "This IBTE role is a new centre — you're building from scratch. Tell me about a time you had to define things from the ground up with no playbook."

---

## Answer

When I joined the MyDish platform team at Dish, there was no established architecture practice. Services were built ad hoc, no consistent patterns, no documentation. Leadership said "make it better" but gave no specific direction.

I spent the first two weeks mapping the current state. I listed every service, every dependency, every pain point the team reported, and every incident from the last 6 months. I identified the top 3 problems. No API standards causing integration failures. No observability, meaning we were blind in production. No deployment consistency, because everything was manual.

I prioritized observability first. You can't improve what you can't measure. I set up Dynatrace dashboards, added structured logging, and created a health endpoint standard. That alone cut incident response time by half because we could actually see what was breaking.

I then built API standards using OpenAPI templates, error format conventions, and pagination patterns. I didn't mandate them. I built the first template, used it on one service, and showed the team how much faster integration was. Teams adopted it organically.

I didn't wait for permission. I just started doing and showing results. Within 90 days, we had observability on every service, API standards being used by 3 teams, and a CI/CD pipeline template that automated deployments. Leadership didn't ask for a detailed plan upfront. They trusted me because I showed progress, not proposals.

## Delivery Tips

- **"I didn't wait for permission"** — say this plainly, not rebelliously. It shows ownership, not defiance
- **"Showed progress, not proposals"** — this is the key insight. It's why leadership trusted you. Pause before it
- **The 90-day close** — gives the answer a timeframe. Makes it concrete
- **The "top 3 problems" list** — rattle it off crisply. Shows clarity of thought

---

## Follow-Up Questions

- **"How did you decide what to prioritize?"** — Observability was the bottleneck. Without it, I couldn't measure the impact of the other two. It was the foundational layer.
- **"Did anyone resist the standards?"** — One team was already using a different pattern. I didn't force them. When their new team members joined and asked why they weren't using the standard, the team changed on their own.
- **"What would you do differently with more time?"** — I'd formalize the ADR process earlier. The standards existed, but the rationale behind them wasn't documented. New engineers had to re-learn the reasoning.
- **"How does this experience apply to the IBTE role?"** — It's the same problem. A new centre with no patterns. I know how to build the foundation without waiting for direction.
